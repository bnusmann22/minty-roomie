Your Roomie connection network is a trust web built from real co-living relationships. Every time you and a roommate sign and pay a Roommate Agreement, that relationship becomes a verified node in your network — and those nodes reach outward to surface mutual connections that help you and every future match feel safer about each other.

## How the network is structured

Roomie models your connections the same way professional networks map relationships — by degrees. The closer the degree, the more direct and verifiable the relationship.

### 1st-degree connections

A 1st-degree connection is someone you have an **active Roommate Agreement** with. This means you have both co-signed an agreement and the Paystack payment has cleared. These are your confirmed, verified roommates — past or present.

Your 1st-degree connections are the strongest trust signal you can carry on the platform. They represent real commitments, not just chats or matched profiles.

### 2nd-degree connections

A 2nd-degree connection is someone who has an active agreement with one of your 1st-degree connections — your **roommate's roommate**. You haven't lived with them directly, but you share a trusted person in common.

When you browse Discover, Roomie checks your 2nd-degree connections and surfaces them as mutual connections on profile cards, giving you an immediate signal of shared social proximity.

---

## Mutual connections on Discover

When you view a student's profile card in the Discover directory, Roomie checks how many of your 1st-degree connections are also connected to them. If there's an overlap, you'll see a prompt like:

> *"Matched with 2 mutual connections"*

This is one of the most powerful trust cues on the platform. It means that the student you're considering has already co-lived with — or is connected to — people in your own network. You can reach out knowing your mutual connections can vouch for them indirectly.

<Note>
Mutual connections are computed in real time. You don't need to do anything to see them — they appear automatically on profile cards as your network grows.
</Note>

## Your network size

Your profile displays your total network size — the combined count of your 1st and 2nd-degree connections. A larger number signals that you have an established co-living history and a broad circle of trusted student contacts.

Other students looking for roommates in Discover factor in network size when evaluating profiles. A student with five 1st-degree connections and a visible network has a demonstrably stronger record than a brand-new profile with no history.

---

## How a bigger network works in your favour

The relationship between network size and match success is straightforward:

<Steps>
  <Step title="You complete a Roommate Agreement">
    You and your new roommate both become 1st-degree connections of each other, and your network sizes increase.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Mutual connections appear on profiles">
    When either of you browses Discover in the future, students connected to your roommate show up with mutual connection indicators on their cards.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Trust signals increase response rates">
    Students are more likely to accept connection requests and reply to messages from profiles that show mutual connections — it removes the uncertainty of engaging with a total stranger.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Your network compounds over time">
    Each new agreement expands your 2nd-degree reach. Two agreements can surface dozens of 2nd-degree connections depending on how well-connected your roommates are.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## A note on network privacy

Your list of individual 1st-degree connections is not publicly displayed by name. Only the **count** of mutual connections between you and another specific user is shown — and only to that user when they view your profile. This keeps your full network private while still surfacing the trust signals that matter most during matching.
