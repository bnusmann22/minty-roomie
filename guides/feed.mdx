The Feed is Roomie's social bulletin board — a structured, scrollable notice board where students broadcast their roommate needs, respond to others, and start conversations in public before moving to a private chat. Think of it as the organised, searchable version of the campus group chat, but built specifically for finding a place to live.

## How the Feed Differs from Discover

The Discover directory shows you **student profiles** — structured records of who someone is, what their preferences are, and how compatible they are with you. The Feed shows you **posts** — short announcements that a student has written to describe exactly what they are looking for right now.

| | Discover | Feed |
|---|---|---|
| **Format** | Profile cards with structured data | Short-form posts with free text |
| **Best for** | Browsing all available students systematically | Finding students with a specific, immediate need |
| **Filtering** | City, university, budget, verification, lifestyle tags | Location, university, date posted |
| **Action** | Send a connection request from the profile | React, comment, or send a connection request from the post |

Using both together gives you the best coverage: Discover surfaces matches you might not have found on your own, while the Feed lets you catch students who are actively broadcasting their situation.

## Browsing Feed Posts

When you open the Feed, you see the latest roommate request posts from students on the platform. To narrow the list:

- **Filter by location** — Show posts from students looking in a specific city or campus area
- **Filter by university** — Limit posts to your institution or nearby schools
- **Sort by date** — Surface the most recent posts first to find active, time-sensitive requests

Tap any post to expand it and read the full description, see the poster's profile snippet, and view any comments or reactions the post has received.

## How to Create a Post

<Steps>
  <Step title="Tap the compose button">
    On the Feed screen, tap the **+ Post** button (or the pencil icon) at the bottom of the screen to open the post editor.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fill in the key details">
    Add the information other students need to decide whether to reach out:
    - **Location / City** — Where you are looking to live
    - **University** — Your institution and campus
    - **Budget** — Your monthly housing budget range (e.g. ₦40,000 – ₦70,000/month)
    - **Availability Date** — When you need to move in
    - **Description** — A short summary of what you are looking for, your living habits, and anything else relevant (noise tolerance, study hours, guests policy, etc.)
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review and publish">
    Tap **Preview** to check how your post will appear on the Feed, then tap **Post** to publish it. Your post goes live immediately and is visible to all students in the Feed.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Respond to comments and requests">
    You will receive in-app notifications when other students comment on or react to your post. If someone wants to connect, they can send you a connection request directly from the post. Review their profile and accept or decline from your Notifications tab.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Reacting and Commenting on Posts

You can engage with any post in the Feed:

- **React** — Tap the reaction button to leave an emoji response (useful for quick acknowledgements without a full comment)
- **Comment** — Tap **Comment** to leave a public reply visible to everyone who views the post. Use comments to ask clarifying questions or express interest
- **Connect** — Tap **Connect** on the post to send the author a direct connection request, which kicks off the standard [connection request flow](/guides/connecting)

## Tips for Getting More Visibility

<Tip>
  Publishing a post on the Feed does not replace your Discover profile — it amplifies it. Students who spot your post and like what they read will often tap through to your full profile to check your compatibility score and lifestyle details. Keep both your post and your profile up to date for the best results.
</Tip>

<Tip>
  Be specific in your post description. Mentioning your study schedule, your stance on overnight guests, or your preferred noise level helps the right people self-select — and discourages mismatches from reaching out. A detailed post saves everyone time.
</Tip>
