When you browse the Discover directory, every profile card shows a compatibility score — a single percentage that summarises how well you and that student are likely to get along as roommates. Instead of guessing whether a stranger's habits will clash with yours, you get an upfront, data-driven signal built from the preferences both of you set during profile setup.

## Where the Score Appears

The compatibility score is displayed prominently on every profile card in the Discover directory. It also appears at the top of a student's full profile page when you tap to view their details. The score updates automatically whenever either of you edits your preferences.

## The Five Scoring Dimensions

Roomie evaluates compatibility across five weighted dimensions. Each one targets a common source of roommate conflict, so the overall score reflects real-world livability — not just surface-level similarities.

| Dimension | What It Measures | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Budget Overlap** | How much your monthly budget ranges overlap | Mismatched budgets lead to housing options one side can't afford |
| **Sleep Schedule** | Alignment between early-riser and night-owl preferences | Opposite sleep times cause noise and routine conflicts |
| **Cleanliness Level** | How closely your cleanliness slider settings match | Differing standards for tidiness are one of the top causes of roommate friction |
| **Location Proximity** | Whether you're targeting the same city or university area | Living too far apart makes shared housing impractical |
| **Lifestyle Tags** | The number of shared tags (e.g. `quiet`, `no_smoking`, `pets_ok`) | Shared habits and house rules reduce day-to-day tension |

## How the Overall Score Is Calculated

Roomie computes your compatibility score as a **weighted average** of the five dimensions above. Each dimension contributes a different proportion to the final score, reflecting how much it typically affects co-living success in a Nigerian student context.

For example, budget overlap and location carry more weight because a mismatch in either category makes sharing a space practically impossible — no matter how aligned your lifestyles are. Sleep schedule and cleanliness carry moderate weight because they affect daily quality of life most directly. Lifestyle tags act as a fine-tuning layer that pushes good matches higher and flags potential friction points.

The result is a single percentage — **0 % to 100 %** — shown on the profile card.

## What the Score Means

| Score Range | What It Indicates |
|---|---|
| **80 % – 100 %** | Strong match across most or all dimensions — a great candidate to connect with |
| **60 % – 79 %** | Good alignment with a few minor differences — worth reviewing the full profile |
| **40 % – 59 %** | Moderate compatibility — check which dimensions are dragging the score before connecting |
| **Below 40 %** | Significant mismatches — proceed carefully and read the full profile thoroughly |

A high score does not guarantee a perfect roommate relationship, but it is a reliable signal that your day-to-day habits and constraints are likely to complement each other. A low score flags areas worth discussing before you commit.

## Keeping Your Score Accurate

<Tip>
  Set your preferences honestly rather than optimistically. If you are a night owl, mark it. If you expect a clean kitchen, move the cleanliness slider accordingly. Accurate inputs produce accurate scores — which means fewer unpleasant surprises after move-in.
</Tip>

You can update your lifestyle preferences, budget range, and lifestyle tags at any time from your profile settings. Your compatibility scores with all other users recalculate automatically after you save changes.
